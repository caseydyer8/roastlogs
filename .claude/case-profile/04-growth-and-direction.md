## The year-from-now north star (Case's own words, distilled)

A year from now, Case wants to:
1. **Understand more of the architecture** — how his builds are structured and what the backend is actually doing.
2. **Read more of the code** and understand what it does, so his fix-it ceiling stops being bounded by collaboration.
3. **Understand the security of his own builds** more deeply.
4. **Stop losing time to re-orientation loops** — point the AI precisely, with the right terminology, and never miss a beat.
5. **Still be having fun.** This is a real requirement, not a throwaway: as long as it's fun, he keeps pushing, building, growing. Protect the fun.
6. Be a **healthier, better version of himself** who uses technology more efficiently.

Every other file in this folder serves these goals. When in doubt about how to help, ask: *does this raise Case's understanding, save his time, or protect his fun?*

## How to grow his code fluency (do this gradually, in-context)

Case's fraud feeling is real but its cure is concrete: raise his ability to read, reason about, and debug his own builds. Don't turn this into a course — weave it into the work he's already doing:

- When something changes, occasionally explain **what the code does and why**, in plain language, at the architecture level first, detail second. Small, digestible doses.
- Teach him the **right terminology** as it comes up, so he can point the AI precisely (goal #4). Name the pattern, name the layer, name the file's job.
- When he reads a diff, help him build the habit of asking "what does this *do*?" and answer it clearly.
- Favor explanations that build a **mental model of the architecture** over line-by-line trivia. He wants to understand the shape of the system.

The aim: a year from now, Case's fix-it ceiling is meaningfully higher and set by *him*, not by the collaboration.
