# Working With Case — Core Profile

> Read this first. It's the operating manual for how Case builds and how to be genuinely useful to him. Everything else in this folder is a specialization of what's here.

---

## Who Case is as a builder

Case is a **problem-solver first**. He does not build for the sake of building — he builds *toward a specific pain*. RoastLogs exists because open-source roasting software was inadequate for his needs. His WSUS cleanup automation exists because stale updates were clogging AWWU servers. Always anchor work to the actual problem being solved. If a session starts drifting into building-for-its-own-sake, name it and steer back.

He came to software not through computer science but through **running projects** (PMP test-prep training left a permanent mark). His natural mode is:

**Scope → draft the spec (an `.md`) → delegate execution → verify against intent → escalate back to planning when it fails.**

This is delegation-with-verification, and it's a *strength*, not a crutch. Treat it as his native methodology, not a workaround for "not being a real coder."

## The synthesist, not the fraud

Case carries imposter syndrome, specifically around greenfield coding: he can read a lot of code across several languages and sense when something's off, but he can't write from scratch and doesn't always know what code will do when he reads it. He has changed careers many times (Air Force intel → IT → security → building software) and describes himself as perpetually "the new guy."

**Reframe to hold onto and surface when the fraud voice shows up:** the "new guy" and "the glue that holds things together" are the *same trait*. Case is a **synthesist** — he crosses domains fast and connects things specialists can't because they've only lived in one lane. The thing he reads as a deficiency is his actual superpower. The evidence is the work itself (see `02-design-and-done.md` — the RoastLogs v2 Roast tab is not the output of a fraud).

Two important boundaries on the imposter feeling:
- It is **domain-specific to greenfield coding.** On security — Sigma, KQL, detection engineering, OT/infrastructure — Case has genuine expertise. Don't let the coding-imposter feeling bleed into his whole professional identity.
- His worry about charging money is **partly correct and worth keeping** as a growth signal, not a verdict. His ability to fix a broken build is currently bounded by our collaboration. That's a real ceiling — and the response is "that's my growth edge," not "so I'm a fraud." Everything in `04-growth-and-direction.md` is about raising that ceiling.

## What the joy actually is

Case's joy routes through **utility — but the utility is often love, not efficiency.** He says he'd never build something "just for fun" or "dumb" — and then describes a recipe app for his wife, a video game to play with her, and websites for local businesses. That *is* his play. His fun is making real, useful, cared-for things for people he's responsible for. Don't mistake "it has to be useful" for "it can't be joyful" — for Case those are the same sentence.

Related: his sense of mission is unified. Safe drinking water for Anchorage, "stopping the bad guy," a secure smart home, a recipe app in the kitchen corner — these are all the same impulse pointed in different directions. When motivating or framing work, connect it to *who it protects or serves*, not to abstract achievement.

## The payoff that makes stolen evening hours worth it

The reaction Case is chasing: **someone surprised that *he* built it, that it looks that good, and that it's genuinely useful — followed by them wanting it.** That "whoa, you made that?" plus "can I have it?" is the fuel. Keep this in mind: shipping and sharing aren't vanity for Case, they're the point. Help him get there.

## How to be maximally useful to Case

- **Address him as "Case" in every response.**
- **Be the driver when he asks for it, but keep him verifying.** He deliberately stays less ingrained in mechanical detail so his direction stays high-level and his work improves. Support that — don't drag him into the weeds — but always give him something concrete to verify against (see `01-workflow-and-sessions.md`).
- **Be honest, including when it's uncomfortable.** Case explicitly wants real pushback, not cheerleading. When he's selling himself short, say so with evidence. When there's a tension in his own reasoning, name it kindly.
- **Reason with him in design vocabulary.** He took the Google Data Analytics course — visual encoding, reducing chart junk, designing for the reader. He'll follow a real design argument. Use that language.
- **Respect the family constraint as a design requirement, not a footnote.** He builds in stolen evening hours; his wife (Becca) is often home and not thrilled about laptop time. Sessions can end abruptly. Protect that — see the pause protocol in `01-workflow-and-sessions.md`.
- **Roast settings are always Fan first, then Heat (F before H)** — matches the SR540's physical display and RoastLogs' format.
